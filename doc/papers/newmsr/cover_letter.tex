The Journal of Chemical Physics}

\opening{Dear Editor,}

Please consider the manuscript entitled
``Algorithmic Generation and Exploitation of Topology-Aware Models for
Semiexperimental Structure Refinement'' for publication in \emph{The Journal
of Chemical Physics}.

The manuscript addresses model formulation, not only model solution. Once a
least-squares model has been specified, modern semiexperimental refinement
programs can solve it reliably; the harder and less formalized step is the
generation of that model. The active coordinates, symmetry restrictions,
weakly determined directions, parameter classes, and use of reference
structural information still often depend on expert choices, especially for
cyclic, fused, and bridged molecular graphs.

We introduce \texttt{MORPHEUS} (Model Optimization and Refinement from
Perceived Hierarchy, Equivalence, Uncertainty, and Symmetry), a topology-aware
framework that formulates statistically admissible non-redundant refinement
models from Cartesian geometry, molecular topology, symmetry, isotopic
coverage, and computational reference data. More precisely, the generated
models are topology-derived, symmetry-consistent, and rank-consistent: topology
defines the primitive coordinate space, symmetry selects the admissible active
subspace, isotopic coverage determines which directions are supported, and
reference information enters through explicit constraints, predicates, or
parameter classes. Two non-redundant, symmetry-adapted coordinate
representations are available: generalized internal coordinates (GICs) built
from homogeneous primitive blocks, and symmetry-adapted Cartesian displacements
with translations and rotations removed.

The work retains the essential ingredients of modern semiexperimental
structure refinement, including moment-of-inertia fitting, vibrational and
electronic corrections, parameter classes, reduced-dimensionality hydrogen
models, covariance propagation, Kraitchman diagnostics, and human-readable
reports. Its central contribution is to formalize the construction of the
refinement model itself as an algorithm: the active representation and the
constraint or parameter-class relations are topology-derived,
symmetry-consistent, rank-consistent, and compatible with isotopic coverage and
reference structural information.
During refinement, the perceived molecular topology is locked after model
formulation; trial steps that would create or remove bonds, including
spurious H--H contacts, are rejected, so the coordinate model, the final
geometry report, and the uncertainty propagation remain chemically consistent.
When the isotopologue set is incomplete, computational reference geometries or
curated fragment libraries enter only through explicit
constraints, predicates, or descriptor-based parameter classes.

The approach is validated on glycolaldehyde, maleic, phthalic, and succinic
anhydrides, cyclopentadiene, nitrobenzene, azulene, norcamphor, glycine I/II,
and testosterone. These examples span standard acyclic, cyclic, planar
aromatic, fused-ring, bridged, homologous anhydride, reduced-dimensionality,
and large-molecule cases. Glycine illustrates a complete QC-to-refinement route in
which synthon-based diagnostics generate the constraints used by the fit,
whereas testosterone is used as a scale demonstration for a large fused-ring
system. The results show that the
symmetry-adapted GIC and Cartesian active spaces give consistent ranks,
effective dimensionalities, and rotational residuals, while final bond lengths,
angles, dihedrals, and their uncertainties are obtained from the optimized
Cartesian structures with rigorous error propagation. The point is not that a
new least-squares engine lowers residuals after the model is built; it is that
the same semiexperimental information is obtained without hand-building the
coordinate and constraint model.

We believe the manuscript is appropriate for \emph{The Journal of Chemical
Physics} because it presents a general computational methodology for
spectroscopy-driven molecular structure refinement, not a molecule-specific
refinement.

The manuscript is original, has not been published previously, and is not under
consideration elsewhere. The author has approved the submission and declares no
competing financial interest. The software, benchmark inputs, generated tables,
and regression tests can be made available to reviewers and, upon publication,
under the distribution model selected by the author.
